Disclosure first: we run Base Note, a luxury fragrance subscription that ships a 5 ml atomizer — roughly 60 sprays — of designer and niche scents every month for a flat $20. The 32 fragrances ranked below are the 32 in our rotation. That is the conflict of interest, up front: we picked the list, so of course we like everything on it. What we can be honest about is the order, what we could not verify, and which of these you should probably never buy a full bottle of.

If you only read one thing: This answers one narrow question — which should you try first? — not which is best? A fragrance ranks high when it teaches you something about your own taste, covers more than one season, and would be an expensive blind buy. That is why Creed Aventus sits at #1 and Mont Blanc Legend, a scent almost nobody dislikes, sits at #29. Aventus tells you something. Legend does not. Short version: run #1, #3 and #7, one per month, and you will know more about your preferences in 90 days than a year of reading note pyramids would teach you.

How we ranked these 32 (and what we could not verify)

We wanted to anchor this in community vote counts, which aggregate tens of thousands of people with no stake in the answer. We could not get them: both Fragrantica and Parfumo returned HTTP 403 to our requests when we checked in August 2026. Rather than launder a half-remembered number into something that looks like evidence, we dropped them. Go look that data up yourself — it is useful, and it is genuinely not in this article.

What is left is a stated method — three criteria, in this weight order:

1. Does it teach you something? A rotation is calibration. A scent that shows you where your tolerance for sweetness, smoke or projection sits is worth more than one that is merely agreeable. Heaviest weight, and the reason a few well-behaved scents rank low.

2. Versatility, on our own tagging. Every product page carries season and occasion tags; four of each is the maximum. Exactly one fragrance hits both (Versace Eros), and four cover all four seasons. Check any of it yourself.

3. Regret risk at full-bottle price. The more expensive and polarizing a scent is, the more it belongs on a try-first list rather than a buy-first one. This criterion happens to favour our business model — treat it as an argument to evaluate, not a fact to accept.

None of this is scored on a spreadsheet. Where two fragrances sat close together, we asked which one a person would regret never having smelled, and moved that one up. A different rotation, run by different people, would produce a defensibly different order.

Notes, families and seasons come from our product pages; editorial judgement did the rest. For the vocabulary, start with how top, heart and base notes work and our fragrance families explainer.

The top 5

1. Creed Aventus

Pineapple and blackcurrant over smoky birch, landing on oakmoss, musk and ambergris. Every other modern masculine gets compared to it, so smelling it properly is a prerequisite. It is also the most expensive possible mistake here: Creed’s own boutique lists Aventus at $510 (Source: Creed Boutique, 2026). See our honest answer on whether it is worth the money and our guide to the dupes and clones. Try it first. Decide second.

2. Parfums de Marly Layton

Apple and lavender into jasmine and violet, then vanilla, cardamom and guaiac. The easiest expensive-smelling scent to like, which is why it is the one people blind-buy and then over-wear. Parfums de Marly lists the 75 ml at $290 (Source: Parfums de Marly US, 2026); our full Layton review tests whether that holds up.

3. YSL Y Intense

Ginger and juniper up top, sage and geranium in the middle, vetiver, patchouli and cedar underneath. Tagged for all four seasons and for both office and date-night. If you own nothing and want one scent that works in either setting, this is it. It ranks third only because it is easier to predict than the two above.

4. Versace Eros

Mint, green apple and lemon over tonka, ambroxan and vanilla. The only fragrance in our catalogue tagged for four seasons and four occasions. It is also loud, and finding your loudness ceiling is one of the most useful things a first rotation does. If Eros is too much, you have learned something about every sweet-fresh scent on the market. Our explainer on projection, sillage and longevity has the vocabulary.

5. Tom Ford Oud Wood

Oud, rosewood, cardamom and sandalwood settling into amber, tonka and vanilla. The gateway to niche: oud with the medicinal edge sanded off, so you learn what people mean by “oud” without being ambushed by the raw material. Our piece on designer versus niche explains what the label actually buys you.

Best first pick if you have never worn niche

Xerjoff Erba Pura. Sicilian orange, Calabrian bergamot and lemon over fruit, landing on white musk, Madagascar vanilla and amber. Xerjoff is a serious Italian niche house, and Erba Pura is its most likeable release — bright, sweet, warm, and free of the difficulty people expect from “niche.”

For the opposite lesson — niche that is demanding — try Initio Side Effect (rum, cinnamon, tobacco, saffron) or Xerjoff Alexandria II, which stacks rose, cardamom and cumin over oud, orris and civet before reaching the vanilla. Alexandria II is the most complex thing here, and the one we would most discourage buying unsampled.

Best for the office

The office test is not “does it smell good.” It is “does anyone two desks away need an opinion about it.” Four picks, all tagged office:

  • Acqua di Gio Profondo Parfum — sea notes and rosemary over mineral musk. Clean without reading as soap.
  • Parfums de Marly Perseus — grapefruit and green apple into lavender and musk. Expensive-smelling, uncontroversial.
  • Bond No. 9 FiDi — black pepper and mandarin over lotus, nutmeg and cedar. Three seasons, unusual for a Bond.
  • Prada Paradigme — bergamot and airy musks over benzoin and guaiac. Deliberately quiet.

Best for evening

Evening is where the expensive, polarizing, cold-weather material earns its keep: Haltane for saffron and praline over oud and leather, Bvlgari Le Gemme Orom for bourbon vanilla, benzoin and agarwood in a three-note structure that hides nothing, and Spicebomb Extreme for cinnamon, cumin and pipe tobacco. All three are fall and winter on our tagging, and all three are too much in a warm room. A feature in November, a problem in June — the argument for rotating rather than committing.

The market agrees. Spate’s 2026 report finds “Gourmand scents, vanilla, and Arabian-inspired perfumes are among the fastest-growing trends” and that “Pistachio, caramel, honey, and matcha are among the highest-growth notes” (Source: Spate, 2026 Fragrance Report). If sweet and warm is where things are going, #9, #12, #15 and #27 are where you test it on yourself.

What is not on this list, and the closest things that are

Two names people expect on any luxury ranking are not in our rotation. We would rather say so than let the omission look like an oversight.

Whole categories are missing too: no vintage chypres, no true florals, almost no leather. A rotation of 32 teaches range, not completeness.

Maison Francis Kurkdjian Baccarat Rouge 540 is not in the Base Note catalogue. We cannot ship it and will not imply otherwise; we wrote a full piece on why people either love it or cannot stand it. If you are chasing the saffron-and-sweet-woods shape it made famous, the nearest things we carry by note structure are Haltane (saffron and praline over oud and leather) and Le Gemme Orom (bourbon vanilla, benzoin, agarwood). Neither is a dupe — shared vocabulary, not a shared formula.

Le Labo is not in the catalogue either. For the creamy sandalwood-and-cardamom register, Creed Santal (Mysore sandalwood, ginger, tonka, cedarwood) and Tom Ford Oud Wood (sandalwood, rosewood, cardamom) are nearest by notes. Adjacent, not equivalent.

Build your first three-month rotation

Three months is the shortest span that tells you anything real — long enough to stop reacting to novelty. Pick a lane.

  • The calibration rotation (recommended). Aventus, then Y Intense, then Erba Pura. Reference point, workhorse, niche introduction. By month three you will know whether you lean fresh or sweet.
  • The cold-weather rotation. Layton, then Oud Wood, then Haltane — sweet to woody to dark. Our fall guide covers the seasonal logic.
  • The office-safe rotation. Perseus, then Profondo, then FiDi. Three ways to smell expensive without anyone commenting.

One caveat outranks the whole list: the same fragrance smells different on different people. Skin oil, pH and temperature change how a composition evolves, which is why a scent that floored you on a friend goes flat on you — the subject of our pillar piece, why you smell the way you do, and the reason we cannot rank anything for you from here. If your skin runs oily, which notes hold their shape on oily skin is the better filter. And if two picks look interchangeable — Green Irish Tweed and Silver Mountain Water are the usual pair — we ran that head-to-head separately.

The bottom line

Any ranking of 32 fragrances is one house’s opinion arranged in a column. We gave you the criteria, named the input we could not verify, and put four brand-published prices in a table so you can check the try-first argument yourself.
