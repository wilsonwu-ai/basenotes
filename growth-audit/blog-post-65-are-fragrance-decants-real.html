What a decant is

A decant is a small amount of fragrance poured out of a full-size bottle into a smaller container, usually a glass spray atomizer, so you can buy a few milliliters instead of the whole bottle. Because the seller does the pouring, you are trusting the seller. That is why the worries below are reasonable ones.

A checklist for any seller

1. What the listing should say

  • Volume in ml. "A sample" is not a size. You want a number, such as 5 ml.
  • The exact fragrance name. Brands often sell several versions with similar names. If the listing says only "Aventus", ask which one.
  • Concentration. Eau de parfum, eau de toilette and parfum smell and last differently, and the price should match.
  • What the liquid is. The real fragrance or something "inspired by" it. Those are different products.

2. What the vial and label should show

  • The fragrance name on the vial itself, not only on the bag or box.
  • A spray fitting that sits firmly and gives a fine mist, not a dribble.
  • Clear glass, so you can judge the fill.

3. What a correct fill looks like

The liquid in a 5 ml atomizer should look close to full. If the vial looks half full, message the seller and ask rather than assume.

4. How it should be packed

Atomizers can leak if the cap is loose or the vial rattles around in a bag. Look for a snug cap and a padded or boxed package. Open the package over a sink or plate the first time, and if anything leaked, photograph it and contact the seller.

5. Price sanity

Counterfeit perfume is a real market. US Customs and Border Protection's Intellectual Property Rights Seizure Statistics for fiscal year 2025, published in May 2026, list 212,468 counterfeit perfume items seized. Their manufacturer's suggested retail price, meaning the value had the goods been genuine, was $38.8 million.

Common red flags include a price far below retail, packaging that looks off, batch codes that do not match, and sellers reachable only through social-media links.

A quick test: divide the full bottle's retail price by its size in ml, then do the same for the decant. The decant should cost more per ml, because you are paying for the pouring, the atomizer and the small quantity. If it costs far less per ml than a normal retail bottle, ask why before you pay.

6. What to email the seller before ordering

  1. Which exact fragrance and concentration is this?
  2. Is it poured from a full-size bottle, and is it poured when I order or in advance?
  3. How many ml does the vial hold, and how full will it be?
  4. How is it packed to avoid leaks?
  5. If something is wrong when it arrives, who do I contact, and what happens next?

The last question matters most. A seller who answers clearly, in writing, before you pay is easier to trust than one who dodges. Keep the answer.

Which size to buy

Two decant sellers' own buying guides, read in November 2026, give similar advice. Samples of 0.5 to 2 ml run out before you understand how a scent dries down. About 5 ml suits a test lasting several weeks. A full bottle should wait until you have had several good wears. These are sellers' guides, so they have a reason to sell you a decant, but the logic holds. More in our posts on sampling before you buy a full bottle and how long a 5 ml atomizer lasts.
