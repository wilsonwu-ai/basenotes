We tried to earn a little of it back by doing the one thing a self-interested comparison usually avoids: we ran the maths that makes us look worst, published it in the first table, and did not bury it. Every competitor number here was pulled off the vendor's own page on 18 August 2026. Fragrance subscription pricing moves; if you are reading this months later, click through and check.

If you only read one thing: On raw cost per millilitre, Base Note does not win. At $20 for 5 ml we are $4.00/ml, while Scentbird and ScentBox both land at $2.24/ml on their entry plans. The moment you pick something from Creed, Xerjoff, Parfums de Marly or Bond No. 9, the competitors move you into a surcharge tier and their cost per ml climbs past ours — Scentbird's premium picks carry a +$10.00 to +$20.00 add-on, and ScentBox's top tier is $32.95/month. Our number does not move. That is the whole pitch, and it is a narrower pitch than "we are better value."

The number printed on the tile and the number you are actually paying are two different numbers, and the gap almost always lives in a line of small print somebody hoped you would skim.

The reason we are writing this anyway is that the sticker price and the price you pay stop being the same number surprisingly quickly, and the mechanism is not hidden — it is printed right on the product tiles.

On Scentbird's cologne browse page on 18 August 2026, we scrolled until the grid stopped loading new cards. Dozens of the fragrance tiles carried an added-fee tag on the card itself, and they were not a small corner of what we saw. Nor were they exotic outliers. Reading straight off the page:

None of this is deceptive. It is disclosed, it is on the page, and it is a defensible way to run a catalogue that spans a $60 department-store bottle and a $400 niche one. First-month promos make everyone look better than they are

Three of the seven services discount month one, and the discounts are steep enough to distort the decision. Scentbird's first month is $8.97 against a $17.95 standing rate — $1.12/ml, half our promotional rate. ScentBox's is $9.72, or $1.22/ml. Ours is $15.00, which is $3.00/ml.

If you subscribe for twelve months, the first month contributes about 8% of what you pay. The promotional price is a customer acquisition cost, not a price. Compute your annual total at the standing rate and ignore the banner. On a twelve-month view: Scentbird entry is $206.42, ScentBox Standard is $207.17, ScentBox Platinum is $380.29, Olfactif is $264.00, and Base Note is $235.00. Add a $10.00 Select pick to six of those Scentbird months and it becomes $266.42.

What cost per ml does not measure

Three things, and they are the reason we think this metric is useful but not sufficient. It is a good first filter and a poor final answer, in roughly the way that price per kilogram is a sensible way to compare bags of rice and a useless way to compare bottles of wine. The unit only works when the contents are interchangeable, and in fragrance they never are.

1. Whether you finish the vial

An 8 ml vial of something you decided you disliked on day four is worse value than a 5 ml of something you wore out. The denominator in cost per ml assumes you use all of it. Most people do not, which is exactly why sampling exists at all — the point of the format is avoiding a $300 mistake, not minimising the cost of the sample.

2. What is in the vial

A cost-per-ml table treats every millilitre as identical, and they are not. A millilitre of Xerjoff sitting on a Sicilian citrus and Madagascar vanilla structure costs the house considerably more to make than a millilitre of a mass-market flanker. This is also why concentration matters more than most comparison tables admit: an eau de toilette and an eau de parfum are not the same liquid, and an eau de toilette generally carries a lower share of fragrance oil, so an identical millilitre delivers less of the thing you are actually buying. We list concentration and country of origin on every product page for that reason.

3. Your skin

The variable nobody prices. The same fragrance costs you more per wearing if it dies on you in two hours, and skin chemistry genuinely moves that. We wrote the long version in why you smell the way you do; the short version is that the same 5 ml can be four hours of wear or nine depending on who is wearing it.

Pick Scentbird or ScentBox if you mostly want mainstream designer fragrances and volume per dollar is the goal.
