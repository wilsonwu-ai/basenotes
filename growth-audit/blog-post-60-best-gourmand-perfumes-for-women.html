What "gourmand" means

A gourmand perfume smells edible. The note list leans on things like vanilla, praline (a caramelized nut), caramel, coffee and cocoa, sometimes on top of flowers and woods. Sugar is the main dial, but not the only one. Coffee and almond can add a bitter edge, and lavender or fruit can add air.

Perfume notes arrive in layers: top notes first, heart notes in the middle, base notes last. In most of these seven, the sweet notes sit in the base, so the sugar shows up after the opening, not during it. These are often shelved as women's perfumes, but anyone can wear them. Base Note's catalog tags Initio Absolute Aphrodisiac and Tom Ford Tobacco Vanille as unisex.

The ladder, from airy to dark

1. Guerlain Mon Guerlain

Fragrantica credits Thierry Wasser and Delphine Jelk and dates it to 2017. Its list puts lavender and bergamot on top, iris, jasmine sambac and rose in the heart, and Tahitian vanilla, sandalwood, licorice and patchouli in the base, among others. Nordstrom's list differs, and I found no official Guerlain pyramid, so treat the list as a guide. On paper, vanilla is one base note among many and lavender leads, which makes this the least sugary rung. If you are new to gourmands, I would start here. Base Note's catalog tags it for office and everyday wear.

2. Valentino Donna Born in Roma

Fragrantica dates it to 2019. The top is black currant, pink pepper and bergamot. The heart is three kinds of jasmine (jasmine sambac, jasmine and jasmine tea), and the base is bourbon vanilla and guaiac wood, among others. No source I found names the perfumer. With a jasmine heart and one vanilla base note, it sits just above rung one: floral first, warm second. The catalog tags it for office and for all four seasons, which makes it the most flexible rung here.

3. Carolina Herrera Good Girl

Fragrantica credits Louise Turner and Quentin Bisch and dates it to 2016. Almond and coffee open it, tuberose (a creamy white flower), jasmine sambac and orange blossom form the heart, and tonka bean, cacao, vanilla and praline sit in the base. Carolina Herrera's own page gives a shorter list, with almond on top, jasmine sambac and tuberose in the heart, and tonka bean and cocoa at the base. On paper, the coffee and almond add a bitter edge, so the sweetness should read as roasted rather than sugary. It suits evenings and dinners, and the catalog tags it for date night.

4. Lancôme La Vie Est Belle

Fragrantica dates it to 2012, and Basenotes gives the same notes. Black currant and pear open it, iris, jasmine and orange blossom form the heart, and praline, vanilla, patchouli and tonka bean sit in the base. They differ on credit, so I will name only the two perfumers both list, Olivier Polge and Dominique Ropion. This is where the base turns openly dessert-like, with praline and vanilla together, while fruit on top and iris in the heart keep it from tipping into candy. The catalog tags it for casual, everyday and evening wear.

5. Kilian Love, Don't Be Shy

Fragrantica credits Calice Becker and dates it to 2007. It is the only pick here whose list names marshmallow, sugar and caramel: neroli, bergamot, pink pepper and coriander on top, a heart of orange blossom, honeysuckle, jasmine, marshmallow, iris and rose, and a base of sugar, vanilla, caramel, musk, civet and labdanum. Kilian's own listing names neroli, rose and marshmallow as the main notes. That makes it the sweetest rung on paper, one for evenings and date nights. At about $305 for 50 ml, it is a good one to try as a vial before buying a bottle. MicroPerfumes' review of it ends by telling readers to sample before buying a full bottle.

The dark rung: Initio Absolute Aphrodisiac

Initio's own site lists the main notes as white flowers, amber, vanilla, musk and castoreum (an animalic note). I found no layered pyramid, retailers give different launch years, and no source names a perfumer, so I am leaving all three out. On paper, the vanilla sits beside musk and castoreum, so it leans less toward dessert than the rungs above. It is the priciest bottle here, at about $410 for 90 ml, and the catalog tags it unisex and for evenings.

The unisex coda: Tom Ford Tobacco Vanille

Vivir credits Olivier Gillotin and dates it to 2007: tobacco leaf and spice on top, vanilla, cacao, tonka bean and tobacco blossom in the heart, and dried fruits and woody notes in the base. Tom Ford's own page lists tobacco leaf, ginger, spice notes, tonka bean, cocoa and a fruit accord, and gives $300 for 50 ml. The cocoa and vanilla make it a gourmand, but the tobacco keeps it dry. The catalog tags it unisex, and it is one of the three vials in Base Note's Her Fall Warmth Trio, along with Maison Margiela Replica By the Fireplace (EDT) and Xerjoff Alexandria II.

Frequently asked questions

Are gourmand perfumes too sweet for the office?

They can be, depending on the rung and the spray count. Mon Guerlain and Born in Roma are the two the catalog tags for office wear. Wear one spray, and keep Kilian, Good Girl and the darker picks for evenings.

What is the longest-lasting gourmand?

I will not give you an hour count, because longevity depends on your skin and how much you spray. Concentration matters too, but all seven here are eau de parfum (a standard perfume strength), so it does not separate them. Our explainer on longevity and projection shows how to judge it yourself.

How many sprays of a strong gourmand?

Start with one and add a second only if you cannot smell it after a few minutes. The upper rungs are the sweetest on paper, so go easy with them. A 5 ml vial has about 60 sprays, which leaves room to find your number.

Do gourmand perfumes work in warm weather?

Some do, with a lighter hand. The catalog tags Born in Roma for all four seasons and the other rungs for fall and winter. In warm months, reach for Born in Roma and spray less.
