How to pick a scent in five minutes

  1. Start at the Scent Finder. Each line gives a style, such as "Fresh, Citrus" or "Amber, Vanilla", plus the seasons it suits. Start with the season you are in now, and note one for the months ahead.
  2. Want a place to start? Look at the bestseller list. It shows what other customers ordered most, not what will suit you.
  3. Open two or three scent pages. Read the notes, then the time of day and occasion lines.
  4. Check them against your week. If you mostly need something for the office, ignore the evening scents for now.
  5. Choose one, or choose three. A single 5 ml vial is $20. Three in a gift box are $56 as a Build Your Own Box Set.

The catalogue's welcome page makes the underlying point in one line: "You can’t judge a fragrance until it’s on your skin." (page 2). Each vial holds about 60 sprays, so you can wear one for weeks before deciding on a bottle.

The five-slot scent wardrobe

You do not need one perfect perfume, the catalogue says. You need a small rotation that covers your week. It sorts every scent into five slots, with the number of scents listed under each:

  • Fresh Daily (18): bright, clean and easy. Citrus, aquatic and green scents.
  • Office & Work (17): polished and close to the skin, tagged for the office.
  • Date Night & Evening (19): warmer and deeper. Sweet, spiced and ambery.
  • Cold-Weather & Cozy (24): vanilla, tobacco, spice and smoke.
  • Special Occasion (20): weddings, celebrations and black-tie.

Those counts add up to more than 55 because a scent can sit in several slots. Louis Vuitton Imagination, for example, is listed under Fresh Daily, Office & Work and Special Occasion. A simple start, suggested on page 155, is one Fresh Daily scent, one for the office and one for date night. Our post on building a fragrance wardrobe goes deeper on the idea.

How to read a scent page

The first page of each entry names the house, the scent, the country and the section. Under that sit a few style words and the notes pyramid. Notes are the individual smells in a fragrance. The catalogue puts it this way: the top notes fade in minutes, and the heart and base are what you live with, so give a new scent a full day.

The second page holds five things, plus a link to the product page:

  • Season: when it tends to work best.
  • Time of day: daytime or evening.
  • Occasion: everyday, office, date night and so on.
  • The story: who made it, when, and what the scent is like.
  • Why wear it: a short line on how it feels and who it suits.

Three scent pages, walked through

1. Louis Vuitton Imagination (pages 72 to 73)

It is an Eau de Parfum from France, tagged For Him and Fresh, Citrus, Aromatic. The notes run from citron, Calabrian bergamot and Sicilian orange on top, through Tunisian neroli, Nigerian ginger and Ceylon cinnamon, to Chinese black tea, ambroxan (an amber-type note), guaiac wood and olibanum (incense) at the base. Season: spring and summer. Time of day: daytime. Occasion: everyday, office and casual. The story says perfumer Jacques Cavallier Belletrud launched it in 2021, and credits the black tea for keeping it from being just another luxury citrus. It is also number one on the bestseller list. Read together, the page says: a warm-weather, daytime scent for work. In mid-November that makes it one to line up for spring, or to wear indoors at the office now.

2. Bond No. 9 Bleecker Street (pages 99 to 100)

A unisex Eau de Parfum from the USA, tagged Fresh, Fruity, Sweet. Top notes are violet leaf, cassis and thyme. The heart is cedarwood, jasmine and cinnamon. The base is oakmoss, suede, patchouli, amber and vanilla. Season: spring, summer and fall. Time of day: daytime. Occasion: everyday and office. The story ties it to the Manhattan street where the house keeps a boutique, and says it launched in 2005. It is number three among the bestsellers, and it appears in both the Fresh Daily and Office & Work slots.

3. Tom Ford Tobacco Vanille (pages 127 to 128)

A unisex Eau de Parfum from the USA, tagged Spicy, Oriental, Vanilla. It opens with tobacco leaf and ginger, moves to tonka bean, vanilla, cocoa and tobacco flower, and ends on dried fruits and woody notes. Season: fall and winter. Time of day: evening. Occasion: date night and evening out. The story says Olivier Gillotin created it in 2007 and describes the tobacco as sweet and leafy rather than smoky.

Put pages 72 and 127 side by side and the logic is clear. One is daytime and warm weather. The other is evening and cold weather. That is already a two-vial start toward the three-scent week the catalogue suggests, and the pages alone tell you so.
