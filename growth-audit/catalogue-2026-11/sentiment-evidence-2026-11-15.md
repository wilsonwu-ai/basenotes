# Social and buyer sentiment evidence, read 2026-11-15

Every item below came from a page a researcher fetched and read on 2026-11-15. Use ONLY these items for third-party
claims. Reddit, TikTok, Fragrantica, Parfumo, Basenotes forum and Quora could NOT be read (blocked), so no article may
say or imply "Reddit says", "TikTok says" or quote a forum. Say "public reviews" or name the publisher.

Rules for using this file
- A statistic is written with its original publisher, the page it was read on, and the date. "as reported by X, read
  November 2026".
- Scento, Surrender to Chance, CA Perfume and Decant House are other sellers. Never link to them. They may be named
  as "a decant retailer" only; do not name them at all unless the item says you may.
- Individual customer reviews are NOT quoted and not relayed one by one. Describe the recurring theme and give the
  aggregate numbers (rating, review count, share of 1-star) with the date read.
- Competitors are described factually and without insult. No claim that a competitor is a scam or dishonest.
- No links to any page in this file except where marked LINK OK.

## A. Fragrance subscriptions (Trustpilot and one other review site)
A1. Scentbird on Trustpilot (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/scentbird.com, read 2026-11-15): TrustScore 3.7 from
    30,832 reviews; 58% five-star, 20% one-star; 13,955 reviews in the last 12 months. Recurring themes in recent
    one-star reviews: being billed after trying to cancel; a monthly charge higher than the price expected at signup;
    terms not clear up front; price felt high for the amount of fragrance. Recurring praise: variety, the queue.
A2. Scentbird on SmartCustomer (https://www.smartcustomer.com/reviews/scentbird.com, read 2026-11-15): 2.6 out of 5
    from 989 reviews, 36% one-star; reviews dated 2024 to June 2026. Themes: renewal without notice, a scent other
    than the one chosen, slow shipping, refund disputes. Praise: the queue, the pause option, variety.
A3. ScentBox on Trustpilot (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/scentbox.com, read 2026-11-15): 4.2 out of 5 from 423
    reviews, 22% one-star. Themes: late or lost shipments and having to chase support; a few reviewers found small
    bottles weak. Praise: customer service, selection.
A4. The BBB profile for Scentbird showed an A+ rating with 0 customer reviews and 0 complaints (read 2026-11-15), so
    it tells you little either way. Optional; fine to leave out.
Recurring buyer questions: How do I cancel and will I still be charged? Why is the monthly price higher than the
advertised one? Do I get the scent I picked? How long does it take and what if it is lost? Can I pause or skip?

## B. Decants and samples
B1. Decant House on Trustpilot (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/decanthouse.com, read 2026-11-15): 4.8 out of 5 from
    1,336 reviews, 92% five-star. Happy buyers praise: authentic product, clear labels, good atomizers, tight packing
    with no leaks, decanting only after the order is placed. Refer to it as "one large decant retailer", not by name.
B2. The same retailer's 1 and 2 star reviews (16 shown, Aug 2023 to Oct 2026): suspected counterfeit, a decant that
    smelled off, vials that arrived half full, leaking on arrival, a bottle smaller than expected, slow shipping.
    Use as "the complaints that do appear are about ...". Aggregate only.
B3. Size advice from two seller guides (CA Perfume, 2026-10-29; Surrender to Chance, undated): samples of 0.5 to 2 ml
    run out before you understand the dry-down; about 5 ml suits a multi-week test; a full bottle should wait for
    several good wears. Attribute as "decant sellers' own buying guides". No link.
B4. Counterfeits (SECOND-HAND, via a retailer's statistics page read 2026-11-15): US Customs and Border Protection,
    fiscal year 2025: 212,468 counterfeit perfume items seized, worth US$38.8M at retail. The fact-checker will try
    to open the CBP original. Until confirmed, write it as "US Customs and Border Protection figures, as compiled by a
    fragrance retailer and read in November 2026". Common red flags listed on that page: a price far below retail,
    packaging that looks off, batch codes that do not match, sellers reachable only through social-media links.
Recurring buyer questions: Is it real perfume or diluted? Will it leak in the mail? Is the vial the size stated and
how full is it? Does it last as long as the bottle? How many wears is 5 ml? When do I stop sampling and buy a bottle?

## C. Blind buying
C1. Retail Brew, 2026-09-21 (https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/this-fragrance-ceo-says-perfumes-popularity-is-the-new-lipstick-effect):
    Interparfums CEO Jean Madar says shoppers now buy scents without smelling them, through TikTok Shop and Amazon,
    and that his company's e-commerce grew 30 to 40% in the first half; he says people treat fragrance as
    self-expression and own several bottles. This is the ONLY permitted TikTok reference, and only as his statement.
C2. A perfume blog's reader discussion (Undina's Looking Glass, 2024-01-13, 35 comments): commenters describe low hit
    rates on blind buys, selling failures at a loss, and "sample first" as the lesson. Older page; use as colour,
    in one sentence, as "a 2024 perfume-blog discussion". No quote.
C3. Mintel, as cited on a retailer's statistics page (read 2026-11-15): about 30% of US fragrance users will not buy a
    scent they have not smelled. SECOND-HAND; hedge it the same way as B4 or leave it out.

## D. Market and trend reporting (2026)
D1. Retail Dive, 2026-08-24, citing Circana (https://www.retaildive.com/news/fragrance-beauty-sales-growth-first-half-2026/828479/):
    first half of 2026, US prestige fragrance dollar sales up about 6%, average prices up 5%, units flat; mass
    fragrance up 15% in dollars and 7% in units; eau de parfum and parfum concentrations grew at double-digit rates.
    Plain reading: people paid more per bottle and did not buy more bottles. HIGH confidence.
D2. Cosmetics Business, 2026-05-20 (free extract only): Spate's Global Beauty Insights ranks "mini perfume" the top
    beauty trend with a projected 78.4% year-on-year rise in 2026; Google and TikTok searches for it grew 123% in
    2025; Circana puts mini sizes at 38% of US prestige fragrances sold. The piece says minis are now more than trial
    sizes and links them to layering ("scent stacking"). Attribute each number to Spate or Circana "as reported by
    Cosmetics Business in May 2026".
D3. Beauty Independent, 2025-12-22 (2026 outlook; opinion from named industry people): the single signature scent is
    giving way to a rotating "fragrance wardrobe"; discovery sets are an entry point; 100 ml bottles take too long to
    finish and 50 ml may become the standard; willingness to pay $200 or more is under pressure. Opinion, not data.
D4. Spate blog, 2026-04-13 (a trend-data vendor's own post): shoppers search by note first; warm gourmand, tobacco,
    vanilla and oud-vanilla profiles are gaining; lighter citrus and floral profiles are losing share. It names no
    catalogue scent. Do not quote its percentages.
D5. Spate blog, 2026-06-15: dupe content drives a lot of brand-versus-brand searching, and low-priced challenger
    brands compete with $300 bottles in reviews. Vendor post; no figures.
D6. Men and teens. A retailer's statistics page (updated 2026-11-11) cites Circana via WWD: US men's fragrance dollar
    sales up 7% in the first half of 2026, units up 3%, while women's prestige was flat; and Piper Sandler via
    BeautyMatter: 74% of US male teens wear fragrance daily. SECOND-HAND; hedge as "as compiled by a fragrance
    retailer, read November 2026" or leave out.
D7. Glossy, 2024-11-28, "The fragrances teen boys are buying for the holidays" (two years old; say so): names Dior
    Sauvage, Valentino Uomo Born In Roma, Viktor & Rolf Spicebomb and an Armani "Stronger" scent among designer
    picks, and Initio Side Effect, Xerjoff, Creed Aventus and Parfums de Marly among niche names; says warm, spicy,
    woody and tobacco profiles lead and that "masculine vanillas" were having a moment; cites Piper Sandler (2024) for
    teen boys' fragrance spending up 26%. Background only, clearly dated 2024.
D8. Holiday timing (PRIOR YEAR). Chain Drug Review, 2025-12-05, citing Circana: December is about 60% of fourth-quarter
    fragrance sales, the peak is the week before Christmas and Christmas week, and gift sets have been more than 25%
    of fourth-quarter prestige sales. Say "last season" every time.
D9. YouGov, November 2025, as compiled on a retailer's statistics page: 57% of US perfume users spent under $50 on
    their last bottle. SECOND-HAND; hedge or leave out.
