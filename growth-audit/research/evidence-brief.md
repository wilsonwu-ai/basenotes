## 1. The 12 most powerful "a-ha" facts

| # | Plain-English statement | Why it surprises | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sweat itself has no smell. Body odour is made *after* the fact, by bacteria transforming odourless molecules your apocrine glands secrete onto the skin. | People believe they smell *sweat*. They smell bacterial metabolism. | 1, 2, 15 |
| 2 | One bacterial enzyme is the whole story. A cysteine-thiol lyase (gene *ShPatB*) in odour-causing *Staphylococcus hominis* is "necessary and sufficient" for underarm thioalcohol odour. *S. epidermidis* — the dominant staph on skin, living right beside it — cannot do it at all. | Two near-identical neighbours; only one makes you smell. Odour is a species-level trait, not a hygiene score. | 1 |
| 3 | That odour-making gene arrived by horizontal gene transfer into one branch of staphylococci roughly **60 million years ago** — long before humans existed. | Our body odour is older than our species. | 1 |
| 4 | Thioalcohols are present in only **trace amounts** yet are the most pungent volatiles in body odour; 3-methyl-3-sulfanylhexan-1-ol (3M3SH) is the most abundant. | Intensity has nothing to do with quantity. | 1 |
| 5 | Apocrine (odour-precursor) glands are **not** on the palms or soles. Those are the sweatiest sites on the body. | The sweatiest areas are not the smelly ones. Different gland, different job. | 3, 11 |
| 6 | You are born with apocrine glands, but they don't switch on until puberty, likely hormonally driven. | Children genuinely don't have adult body odour — it isn't just hygiene. | 4 |
| 7 | A single DNA letter (ABCC11, rs17822931, 538G>A, Gly180Arg) sets **both** your earwax type and your underarm odour. In a Japanese case-control study, **98.7%** (78/79) of clinically diagnosed strong-odour patients carried the G ("odorous") allele vs **35.4%** of the general-population sample (p < 1.1 × 10⁻²⁴). | Your earwax predicts your armpits. | 16, 17, 18, 27 |
| 8 | **77.8%** of genetically non-odorous white Europeans in a ~17,000-person UK cohort used deodorant anyway; only 4.7% of genetically odorous people skipped it. | Most deodorant use is social habit, not biological need. | 21 |
| 9 | In a controlled diet-swap study, men's underarm odour was rated significantly **more pleasant, more attractive and less intense** on a non-meat diet than a meat-heavy one. | Diet changes how you smell to other people, measurably. | 28 |
| 10 | Across three experiments, men who ate **more garlic** were rated as smelling more pleasant, more attractive and less intense. | The exact opposite of the stereotype. | 29 |
| 11 | Same sweat, same session: **polyester** T-shirts smelled significantly worse than **cotton**, because *Micrococcus* — a malodour species — was isolated almost solely from synthetic shirts. | Your gym shirt is a variable, not a bystander. | 36 |
| 12 | People rated their own body odour blended with their **own chosen** perfume as more pleasant than the same body odour blended with a randomly assigned perfume — even when both perfumes were equally pleasant alone. | The "right" fragrance is a property of the *blend*, not the bottle. This is the article's thesis, and it is peer-reviewed. | 60 |

*Bench (use if space):* 2-nonenal, the greasy/grassy "age" note, was detected **only** in subjects aged 40+ (25). Garlic's allyl methyl sulfide peaks in **skin** emission ~30 min after eating and stays elevated **8+ hours** (30).

## 2. The causal map: gland → bacteria → molecule → smell

| Gland / source | What it secretes | Who acts on it | Molecule produced | Smells like | Src |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eccrine (everywhere; densest palms/soles) | ~99% water, sodium/potassium; enriched in lactate, urea, ammonia | *Cutibacteria*, staphylococci | Short- and medium-chain volatile fatty acids | Sharp, sour, "post-workout" | 2, 41 |
| Apocrine (axilla, groin/perineum, areola, ear canal, scalp) — odourless at secretion | Glutamine-conjugated precursors | *Corynebacterium* (Nα-acylglutamine aminoacylase) | 3M2H, HMHA | Sour, cumin-like, classic "armpit" | 5, 9 |
| Apocrine | Cys-Gly-3M3SH (odourless) | *S. hominis* C-T lyase (ShPatB) | 3M3SH (thioalcohol) | Pungent, onion/sulphurous | 1 |
| Apocrine | Steroid precursors | *Corynebacterium* | 5α-androst-16-en-3α-ol; androstenone | Musky / urinous-sweaty; very low detection threshold | 9, 14 |
| Eccrine, feet | Leucine in sweat | *S. epidermidis*, *Bacillus* spp. | Isovaleric acid | Cheesy feet | 13 *(weak source — see §8)* |
| Sebaceous / skin-surface lipids | Omega-7 fatty acids (palmitoleic, vaccenic) | Oxidation, not bacteria | 2-Nonenal | Greasy, grassy, "aged" | 25, 26 |
| Any apocrine site | Apocrine sweat | *Micrococcaceae*, diphtheroids, propionibacteria | Ammonia, short-chain fatty acids | Acrid, sour | 38 |

Two orthogonal levers: **what your glands hand over** (genetics, diet, hormones) and **which bacteria are holding the knife** (skin microbiome, fabric, washing).

## 3. Genetics & populations — cited, non-stereotyping

**Phrasing rule: describe allele frequencies, never people.** Say "the low-odour variant is common in East Asian populations." Never "East Asians don't smell." Never map a genotype onto a person you are talking to.

- ABCC11 rs17822931: AA = dry earwax + little/no axillary odour; GG/GA = wet earwax + typical odour (16). Mechanism: Gly180Arg changes the transporter protein's function (17).
- Population-genomics (dbSNP/ALFA) allele frequencies for the low-odour A/T allele: **East Asian ~88.0%** (n=8,650), **European ~12.5%** (n=311,314), **African ~3.2%** (n=66,682) (19). It is described as one of the most differentiated nonsynonymous SNPs between East Asian and African populations (20).
- Clinically, the G allele is a **prerequisite** for axillary odour expression, making genotype a usable diagnostic marker (27).
- **Genes are not destiny at the individual level** — lead with fact #8 (21, 22). AA homozygotes were ~5-fold overrepresented among never/infrequent deodorant users, but most still wore it.
- **MHC / "sweaty T-shirt":** 49 women rated shirts worn by 44 men; those not on oral contraceptives preferred odours of men with *dissimilar* immune-system genes (23). Frame as one classic, much-discussed 1995 study — evocative, not settled.
- **Age:** 2-nonenal, detected only in subjects 40+, from oxidative degradation of skin-surface omega-7 fatty acids (25, 26). Write it as chemistry, never as insult.

## 5. Skin × fragrance mechanics — **evidence caveat first**

⚠️ **The skin-chemistry research lane returned no usable evidence.** All six findings in that lane were placeholder test data and are unusable. Everything below comes from **fragrance-industry and editorial sources, not peer-reviewed science.** It must be written as *perfumer convention*, hedged ("perfumers generally find", "industry guidance suggests"), never as fact.

- **Oily skin acts as a fixative** — natural oils bind heavier base notes and slow evaporation, so scent lasts longer and reads more intensely (66). Guidance: reach for astringent citrus (grapefruit, lemon, bergamot); sweet notes can tip "sickly" (72).
- **Dry skin loses top notes fast**, sometimes before the heart opens (67). Guidance: unscented moisturiser as a base layer; favour heavier, slower-evaporating molecules — deep woods, oud, vanilla, sandalwood (68, 73).
- **Skin acidity as an EQ knob**: more acidic skin is said to sharpen citrus/bergamot/herbal tops; less acidic skin softens them and fast-forwards to the base (69). Cited "ideal" skin pH range: **4.7–5.75** (70). *Trade source. Hedge hard.*
- **Warm/active skin**: a working perfumer's rule is that scent molecules travel further in warm air, so **use less in heat** (74); lighter "gauze-like" citrus, herb, fruit and aquatic profiles over heavy florientals (75).
- **The one peer-reviewed anchor**: fragrance interacts with body odour to create an individually specific mixture, and self-chosen perfume outperforms random perfume on the same person (60, 61). Build §5 on this and let the trade guidance sit under it as craft, not science.
- Market proof of the concept: Molecule 01 is built on a single aroma-chemical (Iso E Super) explicitly marketed as combining with the wearer's own smell (62); the "skin scent" category is defined as enhancing natural skin rather than masking it (64). Korean fragrance culture systematises this — musk, clean powder, tea, soft woods, deliberately short projection: *intimacy instead of projection* (85, 86).

## 6. The pairing analogy, mapped to fragrance

**Congruent** = amplify a shared quality. **Contrast** = set an opposing quality against it to restore balance. Both are legitimate professional playbooks in wine and food (58).

The science that makes the analogy honest:
- Sucrose **significantly suppressed** the bitterness of caffeine, quinine, urea, PROP and denatonium, moving each from "moderate" to "weak" (54) — but *never* down to sugar-alone level (55). **Contrast balances; it does not erase.** That is exactly the promise to make about fragrance.
- Tannin astringency is physical: tannins aggregate salivary proline-rich proteins, reducing saliva's lubricity and raising oral friction (51, 52). Sweetness measurably lowers perceived astringency even within a wine (53).
- **The cultural humility line**: Ahn et al.'s analysis of 381 ingredients and 1,021 flavour compounds found Western cuisines favour ingredient pairs *sharing* flavour compounds, while East Asian cuisines *avoid* compound-sharing (56). "Shared molecules = good pairing" is a regional convention, not a law.
- Perfumery already does deliberate contrast: the **chypre** is built as bright citrus/green top against a dark mossy base (oakmoss, patchouli, labdanum) (57).

**Five concrete examples** (fragrance side = perfumer convention, flag as such):

| Your signature | Strategy | Pairing | Logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sour/sharp (VFA-driven, post-workout) | **Contrast** | Soft musk, vanilla, powdery amber | Sweetness suppresses harshness substantially but not completely (54, 55) |
| Warm/sweet, oily skin | **Contrast** | Grapefruit, bergamot, vetiver | Astringent tops cut intensity oily skin adds (72) |
| Sulphurous/pungent (thioalcohol-forward) | **Contrast** | Clean citrus-green top over mossy base — the chypre structure | Bright-against-dark is a proven accord (57) |
| Faint, low-odour (AA genotype), dry skin | **Congruent** | Sandalwood, vanilla, oud | Heavier molecules evaporate slower; nothing to counterbalance (68, 73) |
| Musky/skin-forward | **Congruent** | White musk, clean powder, tea, soft woods | The K-beauty skin-scent logic: enhance, don't mask (85, 86, 64) |

## 8. Facts we must NOT claim

1. **Do not present any skin-chemistry claim as scientific.** The entire skin×fragrance research lane returned six placeholder test entries with fake sources (example.com) and zero real findings. Everything in §5 is trade/editorial. No pH number, no "acidic skin sharpens citrus", no "oily skin holds scent" may be stated as fact.
2. **Do not claim skin pH 4.7–5.75 changes fragrance perception as established science.** Single trade source (70).
3. **Do not state the isovaleric-acid foot-odour mechanism as settled.** Source is a non-peer-reviewed FAQ page (13); confidence self-rated *low*. Either drop it or attribute loosely.
4. **Do not say East Asian people don't have body odour.** Report allele frequencies in populations only (19, 20). Never genotype a reader.
5. **Do not say "genes determine whether you need deodorant."** 77.8% of genetically non-odorous people use it anyway (21).
6. **Do not claim shared molecules make a good pairing universally.** East Asian cuisines do the opposite deliberately (56).
7. **Do not claim sugar/soft notes "cancel" or "neutralise" harshness.** Suppression was substantial but never complete (55). Use "balance", never "eliminate".
8. **Do not claim garlic makes you smell bad.** Three studies found the opposite for body odour (29). Breath is a separate question.
9. **Do not claim keto breath is harmless full stop.** Fruity/acetone breath overlaps with diabetic ketoacidosis; the "peaks in 2–4 weeks" figure is from a commercial blog with *low* confidence (35). Direct readers to a clinician.
10. **Never position fragrance as a solution for TMAU.** Trimethylaminuria is an autosomal-recessive FMO3 disorder (chr 1q24.3) causing fishy odour in breath, sweat and urine — a diagnosis, not a pairing problem (24, 76, 77). Same for intertrigo (40) and diabetic ketoacidosis. Include a plain "see a doctor" line.
11. **Do not overstate the MHC t-shirt study.** One 1995 study, 49 raters, effect limited to women not on oral contraceptives (23).
12. **Do not claim a "personal scent analysis" market size.** No firm has published one; only the broader K-beauty figure exists ($118.3B in 2025 → $252.4B by 2033, 10.0% CAGR) (90).
13. **Do not present androstenone's "boar taint" link as meaning humans smell like pork.** Chemical relatedness only (14).
