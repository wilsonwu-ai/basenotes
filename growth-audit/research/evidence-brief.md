## 1. The 12 most powerful "a-ha" facts

| # | Plain-English statement | Why it surprises | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sweat itself has no smell. Body odour is made *after* the fact, by bacteria transforming odourless molecules your apocrine glands secrete onto the skin. | People believe they smell *sweat*. They smell bacterial metabolism. | 1, 2, 15 |
| 2 | One bacterial enzyme is the whole story. A cysteine-thiol lyase (gene *ShPatB*) in odour-causing *Staphylococcus hominis* is "necessary and sufficient" for underarm thioalcohol odour. *S. epidermidis* — the dominant staph on skin, living right beside it — cannot do it at all. | Two near-identical neighbours; only one makes you smell. Odour is a species-level trait, not a hygiene score. | 1 |
| 3 | That odour-making gene arrived by horizontal gene transfer into one branch of staphylococci roughly **60 million years ago** — long before humans existed. | Our body odour is older than our species. | 1 |
| 4 | Thioalcohols are present in only **trace amounts** yet are the most pungent volatiles in body odour; 3-methyl-3-sulfanylhexan-1-ol (3M3SH) is the most abundant. | Intensity has nothing to do with quantity. | 1 |
| 5 | Apocrine (odour-precursor) glands are **not** on the palms or soles. Those are the sweatiest sites on the body. | The sweatiest areas are not the smelly ones. Different gland, different job. | 3, 11 |
| 6 | You are born with apocrine glands, but they don't switch on until puberty, likely hormonally driven. | Children genuinely don't have adult body odour — it isn't just hygiene. | 4 |
| 7 | A single DNA letter (ABCC11, rs17822931, 538G>A, Gly180Arg) sets **both** your earwax type and your underarm odour. In a Japanese case-control study, **98.7%** (78/79) of clinically diagnosed strong-odour patients carried the G ("odorous") allele vs **35.4%** of the general-population sample (p < 1.1 × 10⁻²⁴). | Your earwax predicts your armpits. | 16, 17, 18, 27 |
| 8 | **77.8%** of genetically non-odorous white Europeans in a ~17,000-person UK cohort used deodorant anyway; only 4.7% of genetically odorous people skipped it. | Most deodorant use is social habit, not biological need. | 21 |
| 9 | In a controlled diet-swap study, men's underarm odour was rated significantly **more pleasant, more attractive and less intense** on a non-meat diet than a meat-heavy one. | Diet changes how you smell to other people, measurably. | 28 |
| 10 | Across three experiments, men who ate **more garlic** were rated as smelling more pleasant, more attractive and less intense. | The exact opposite of the stereotype. | 29 |
| 11 | Same sweat, same session: **polyester** T-shirts smelled significantly worse than **cotton**, because *Micrococcus* — a malodour species — was isolated almost solely from synthetic shirts. | Your gym shirt is a variable, not a bystander. | 36 |
| 12 | People rated their own body odour blended with their **own chosen** perfume as more pleasant than the same body odour blended with a randomly assigned perfume — even when both perfumes were equally pleasant alone. | The "right" fragrance is a property of the *blend*, not the bottle. This is the article's thesis, and it is peer-reviewed. | 60 |

*Bench (use if space):* 2-nonenal, the greasy/grassy "age" note, was detected **only** in subjects aged 40+ (25). Garlic's allyl methyl sulfide peaks in **skin** emission ~30 min after eating and stays elevated **8+ hours** (30).

## 3. Genetics & populations — cited, non-stereotyping

**Phrasing rule: describe allele frequencies, never people.** Say "the low-odour variant is common in East Asian populations." Never "East Asians don't smell." Never map a genotype onto a person you are talking to.

- ABCC11 rs17822931: AA = dry earwax + little/no axillary odour; GG/GA = wet earwax + typical odour (16). Mechanism: Gly180Arg changes the transporter protein's function (17).
- Population-genomics (dbSNP/ALFA) allele frequencies for the low-odour A/T allele: **East Asian ~88.0%** (n=8,650), **European ~12.5%** (n=311,314), **African ~3.2%** (n=66,682) (19). It is described as one of the most differentiated nonsynonymous SNPs between East Asian and African populations (20).
- Clinically, the G allele is a **prerequisite** for axillary odour expression, making genotype a usable diagnostic marker (27).
- **Genes are not destiny at the individual level** — lead with fact #8 (21, 22). AA homozygotes were ~5-fold overrepresented among never/infrequent deodorant users, but most still wore it.
- **MHC / "sweaty T-shirt":** 49 women rated shirts worn by 44 men; those not on oral contraceptives preferred odours of men with *dissimilar* immune-system genes (23). Frame as one classic, much-discussed 1995 study — evocative, not settled.
- **Age:** 2-nonenal, detected only in subjects 40+, from oxidative degradation of skin-surface omega-7 fatty acids (25, 26). Write it as chemistry, never as insult.

## 5. Skin × fragrance mechanics — **evidence caveat first**

⚠️ **The skin-chemistry research lane returned no usable evidence.** All six findings in that lane were placeholder test data and are unusable. Everything below comes from **fragrance-industry and editorial sources, not peer-reviewed science.** It must be written as *perfumer convention*, hedged ("perfumers generally find", "industry guidance suggests"), never as fact.

- **Oily skin acts as a fixative** — natural oils bind heavier base notes and slow evaporation, so scent lasts longer and reads more intensely (66). Guidance: reach for astringent citrus (grapefruit, lemon, bergamot); sweet notes can tip "sickly" (72).
- **Dry skin loses top notes fast**, sometimes before the heart opens (67). Guidance: unscented moisturiser as a base layer; favour heavier, slower-evaporating molecules — deep woods, oud, vanilla, sandalwood (68, 73).
- **Skin acidity as an EQ knob**: more acidic skin is said to sharpen citrus/bergamot/herbal tops; less acidic skin softens them and fast-forwards to the base (69). Cited "ideal" skin pH range: **4.7–5.75** (70). *Trade source. Hedge hard.*
- **Warm/active skin**: a working perfumer's rule is that scent molecules travel further in warm air, so **use less in heat** (74); lighter "gauze-like" citrus, herb, fruit and aquatic profiles over heavy florientals (75).
- **The one peer-reviewed anchor**: fragrance interacts with body odour to create an individually specific mixture, and self-chosen perfume outperforms random perfume on the same person (60, 61). Build §5 on this and let the trade guidance sit under it as craft, not science.
- Market proof of the concept: Molecule 01 is built on a single aroma-chemical (Iso E Super) explicitly marketed as combining with the wearer's own smell (62); the "skin scent" category is defined as enhancing natural skin rather than masking it (64). Korean fragrance culture systematises this — musk, clean powder, tea, soft woods, deliberately short projection: *intimacy instead of projection* (85, 86).

## 6. The pairing analogy, mapped to fragrance

**Congruent** = amplify a shared quality. **Contrast** = set an opposing quality against it to restore balance. Both are legitimate professional playbooks in wine and food (58).

The science that makes the analogy honest:
- Sucrose **significantly suppressed** the bitterness of caffeine, quinine, urea, PROP and denatonium, moving each from "moderate" to "weak" (54) — but *never* down to sugar-alone level (55). **Contrast balances; it does not erase.** That is exactly the promise to make about fragrance.
- Tannin astringency is physical: tannins aggregate salivary proline-rich proteins, reducing saliva's lubricity and raising oral friction (51, 52). Sweetness measurably lowers perceived astringency even within a wine (53).
- **The cultural humility line**: Ahn et al.'s analysis of 381 ingredients and 1,021 flavour compounds found Western cuisines favour ingredient pairs *sharing* flavour compounds, while East Asian cuisines *avoid* compound-sharing (56). "Shared molecules = good pairing" is a regional convention, not a law.
- Perfumery already does deliberate contrast: the **chypre** is built as bright citrus/green top against a dark mossy base (oakmoss, patchouli, labdanum) (57).

**Five concrete examples** (fragrance side = perfumer convention, flag as such):

| Your signature | Strategy | Pairing | Logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sour/sharp (VFA-driven, post-workout) | **Contrast** | Soft musk, vanilla, powdery amber | Sweetness suppresses harshness substantially but not completely (54, 55) |
| Warm/sweet, oily skin | **Contrast** | Grapefruit, bergamot, vetiver | Astringent tops cut intensity oily skin adds (72) |
| Sulphurous/pungent (thioalcohol-forward) | **Contrast** | Clean citrus-green top over mossy base — the chypre structure | Bright-against-dark is a proven accord (57) |
| Faint, low-odour (AA genotype), dry skin | **Congruent** | Sandalwood, vanilla, oud | Heavier molecules evaporate slower; nothing to counterbalance (68, 73) |
| Musky/skin-forward | **Congruent** | White musk, clean powder, tea, soft woods | The K-beauty skin-scent logic: enhance, don't mask (85, 86, 64) |

## 8. Facts we must NOT claim

1. **Do not present any skin-chemistry claim as scientific.** The entire skin×fragrance research lane returned six placeholder test entries with fake sources (example.com) and zero real findings. Everything in §5 is trade/editorial. No pH number, no "acidic skin sharpens citrus", no "oily skin holds scent" may be stated as fact.
2. **Do not claim skin pH 4.7–5.75 changes fragrance perception as established science.** Single trade source (70).
3. **Do not state the isovaleric-acid foot-odour mechanism as settled.** Source is a non-peer-reviewed FAQ page (13); confidence self-rated *low*. Either drop it or attribute loosely.
4. **Do not say East Asian people don't have body odour.** Report allele frequencies in populations only (19, 20). Never genotype a reader.
5. **Do not say "genes determine whether you need deodorant."** 77.8% of genetically non-odorous people use it anyway (21).
6. **Do not claim shared molecules make a good pairing universally.** East Asian cuisines do the opposite deliberately (56).
7. **Do not claim sugar/soft notes "cancel" or "neutralise" harshness.** Suppression was substantial but never complete (55). Use "balance", never "eliminate".
8. **Do not claim garlic makes you smell bad.** Three studies found the opposite for body odour (29). Breath is a separate question.
9. **Do not claim keto breath is harmless full stop.** Fruity/acetone breath overlaps with diabetic ketoacidosis; the "peaks in 2–4 weeks" figure is from a commercial blog with *low* confidence (35). Direct readers to a clinician.
10. **Never position fragrance as a solution for TMAU.** Trimethylaminuria is an autosomal-recessive FMO3 disorder (chr 1q24.3) causing fishy odour in breath, sweat and urine — a diagnosis, not a pairing problem (24, 76, 77). Same for intertrigo (40) and diabetic ketoacidosis. Include a plain "see a doctor" line.
11. **Do not overstate the MHC t-shirt study.** One 1995 study, 49 raters, effect limited to women not on oral contraceptives (23).
12. **Do not claim a "personal scent analysis" market size.** No firm has published one; only the broader K-beauty figure exists ($118.3B in 2025 → $252.4B by 2033, 10.0% CAGR) (90).
13. **Do not present androstenone's "boar taint" link as meaning humans smell like pork.** Chemical relatedness only (14).

## 5-ADDENDUM. Skin × fragrance — RE-RUN LANE (real evidence; supersedes the caveat in §5)

**Summary:** Skin chemistry genuinely changes how a fragrance smells, and the mechanisms are traceable. Skin surface pH sits acidic (roughly 4.1-5.8, averaging ~4.7-5.5) and governs hydrolysis rates of ester/aldehyde fragrance materials, so the same molecule releases at a different rate on different people. Sebum acts as a lipid reservoir that binds and slow-releases fragrance oils (oilier skin = longer wear), and a 2025 peer-reviewed evaporation study (10 volunteers, instrumented skin measurements) confirms skin hydration, transepidermal water loss, and surface roughness measurably change fragrance evaporation profiles versus an inert surface. Skin temperature raises vapor pressure exponentially (Clausius-Clapeyron), so warmer skin/pulse points diffuse scent faster and compress the top/heart/base timeline. The strongest evidence is psychological/genetic: Lenochova et al. (2012, PLoS ONE) show perfume interacts with an individual's own body odor to create a unique blend, and people's own chosen perfume is rated more attractive on their own skin than an identical-quality perfume assigned at random - refuting simple 'masking.' Milinski & Wedekind (2001) found perfume preference correlates with MHC immune genotype for self-selection specifically. Allen et al. (2016) and Sorokowska et al. (2016) show fragrance changes what mate-quality/personality cues raters can extract from body odor. Skin bacteria (e.g., Staphylococcus hominis) enzymatically convert odorless precursors into odorous compounds, which is why the fragrance industry (Givaudan, Firmenich) now designs microbiome-aware and bacteria-activated fragrances. Hormones move the needle too: menstrual cycle phase and oral contraceptive use measurably shift a woman's ability to discriminate body odors (19-23% swings). Nose-blindness to one's own perfume is real and even measurable in lab tests within about 15-30 minutes. Concentration (parfum > EDP > EDT) is a real, industry-standard determinant of longevity. Diet's effect is real but thinly sourced in what we could independently verify. Blood type as a determinant has no evidence base in the literature searched; it is folk claim, not chemistry.

- **[S1]** Healthy human skin surface pH is acidic, averaging around 4.7-5.5 with a broader measured range of about 4.1-5.8 across individuals; this 'acid mantle' is maintained by sweat, sebum, and microbial metabolites. — *review-article, 2022*, conf high. Quote: "Recent investigations measuring skin surface pH in healthy volunteers report an average pH between 4.1 and 5.8." — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9135183/
  - ELI5: Your skin is naturally a little bit acidic, like weak vinegar, and that acidity level is different from person to person.
- **[S2]** Skin pH varies by sex (tends to run below 5 in men, above 5 in women) and by modifiable factors including cleansers, sweat, sebum, and diet - all of which shift the chemical environment fragrance molecules sit in. — *review-article, 2017*, conf high. Quote: "pH value below 5 in men and above 5 in women... Modifiable factors [include] cleansers, topicals, sweat, sebum, and diet." — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5605222/
  - ELI5: Men's and women's skin tends to sit at slightly different acidity levels, and things like soap, sweat, and what you eat can nudge that acidity up or down.
- **[S3]** Because fragrance pH governs the rate of ester/aldehyde hydrolysis on skin, a perfume genuinely undergoes chemical transformation on contact with skin rather than sitting inert - a full pH-unit difference between two people's skin changes how fast bonds in fragrance molecules break down. — *industry, 2025*, conf medium. Quote: "This matters because pH governs the rate of hydrolysis, the cleavage of chemical bonds by water." — https://premierepeau.com/blogs/news/what-the-ph-of-your-skin-does-to-a-perfumers-formula
  - ELI5: Perfume doesn't just sit on top of your skin - your skin's acidity actually helps break the scent molecules apart, and more acidic skin does this faster.
- **[S4]** Sebum (skin's natural oil) acts as the primary lipid-soluble binding/reservoir medium for fragrance molecules, so oilier skin builds a thicker lipid film that captures and slow-releases scent. — *industry, 2025*, conf medium. Quote: "Sebum, the waxy, lipid-rich substance produced by sebaceous glands, is the primary binding agent for fragrance molecules on skin." — https://premierepeau.com/blogs/news/body-chemistry-perfume
  - ELI5: The natural oil on your skin acts like a sponge that soaks up perfume and lets it out slowly, so oilier skin can hold scent longer.
- **[S5]** A 2025 peer-reviewed instrumented study measured fragrance evaporation directly on 10 volunteers' skin (vs. an inert surface) and found two distinct behavior patterns: more-volatile fragrance compounds evaporate faster with higher skin surface roughness, while less-volatile, more lipophilic compounds are driven by skin hydration and transepidermal water loss (TEWL). — *peer-reviewed, 2025*, conf medium. Quote: "Two patterns of behaviour have been identified among fragrance compounds: the more volatile compounds whose evaporation increases with surface skin roughness and the less volatile and more lipophilic compounds influenced by skin hydration and TEWL." — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ics.13085
  - ELI5: Scientists actually measured how fast perfume evaporates off real people's skin, and found that skin texture matters for 'light' scent notes while skin moisture matters for 'heavy' notes.
- **[S6]** Vapor pressure of a liquid rises non-linearly (exponentially) as temperature increases - the physical basis (Clausius-Clapeyron relation) for why warmer skin makes fragrance molecules evaporate and diffuse into the air faster. — *textbook, 2023*, conf high. Quote: "The vapor pressure curves of most liquids have similar shapes with the vapor pressure steadily increasing as the temperature increases." — https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Physical_and_Theoretical_Chemistry_Textbook_Maps/Supplemental_Modules_(Physical_and_Theoretical_Chemistry)/Physical_Properties_of_Matter/States_of_Matter/Phase_Transitions/Clausius-Clapeyron_Equation
  - ELI5: Warm things evaporate faster than cool things, and the hotter something gets, the faster this speeds up - which is why perfume 'blooms' more on warm skin or pulse points.
- **[S7]** Perfume notes are conventionally classified by how quickly they evaporate: top notes are small, light molecules that evaporate fast and form the first impression, while base notes are typically not perceived until roughly 30 minutes after application. — *other, 2026*, conf medium. Quote: "Compounds of this class of scents are typically rich and 'deep' and are usually not perceived until 30 minutes after application." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfume
  - ELI5: A perfume changes over time because its ingredients evaporate at different speeds - light 'top note' smells hit you first, heavy 'base note' smells show up later.
- **[S8]** Fragrance concentration categories carry real, industry-consistent oil percentages: eau de toilette ~5-15%, eau de parfum ~15-20%, and parfum/extrait ~20-30% (up to IFRA's informal ~20% reference point for extrait), which is why higher concentrations generally last longer on skin. — *reputable-press, 2025*, conf high. - **[S9]** In controlled experiments, the interaction between an individual's own body odor and a perfume was highly significant for every rated variable (pleasantness, attractiveness, intensity) - and this interaction effect was consistently stronger than the perfume's general effect alone, refuting the idea that perfume simply 'masks' body odor. — *peer-reviewed, 2012*, conf high. Quote: "The interaction between individual donors and perfume treatment was highly significant for all rated variables." — https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0033810
  - ELI5: The same perfume blends differently with each person's natural body smell, and that blend - not just the perfume alone - is what people are actually reacting to.
- **[S10]** In the same study, when men wore their own self-selected perfume on one armpit and a randomly assigned perfume of equal general pleasantness on the other, female raters found the own-perfume blend significantly more attractive (p=0.02) and pleasant (p=0.01) - direct evidence that people are choosing perfumes that interact well specifically with their own body odor. — *peer-reviewed, 2012*, conf high. Quote: "The odor blends with the participants' own perfume were rated significantly more attractive (Z = 2.37; N = 21; p = 0.02) and pleasant (Z = 2.48; N = 21; p = 0.01) than blends with the assigned perfume." — https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0033810
  - ELI5: When men wore their favorite perfume vs. a random one of similar quality, people liked the smell of their favorite better - because it actually goes with that person's own scent, not because the perfume itself was objectively nicer.
- **[S11]** Individual perfume-ingredient preference correlates with a person's MHC (major histocompatibility complex, immune-related) genotype specifically for scents rated 'for self' - but this correlation essentially disappears for scents rated 'for a partner,' suggesting people select perfume to amplify their own body-odor signal rather than to attract others. — *peer-reviewed, 2001*, conf high. Quote: "An overall analysis showed a significant correlation between the MHC and the scorings of the scents 'for self' in both tests... Hardly any significant correlation between MHC genotype and ratings of the scents 'for partner' were found." — https://academic.oup.com/beheco/article-abstract/12/2/140/239863
  - ELI5: The specific immune-system genes you carry are linked, in a small but measurable way, to which perfume ingredients you personally like to wear on yourself - but not to what you'd pick for someone else.
- **[S12]** Wearing artificial fragrance disrupts people's ability to accurately read biologically-relevant cues from body odor: female raters' assessment of male facial vs. body-odor masculinity normally correlate, but that correlation vanished when the body odor was blended with fragrance - an effect specific to female raters judging male odor. — *peer-reviewed, 2016*, conf high. Quote: "The correlation between female ratings of male facial and odor masculinity was not evident when assessing a body odor and fragrance blend." — https://www.stir.ac.uk/research/hub/publication/574255
  - ELI5: Perfume doesn't just add a smell on top - it can actually scramble the natural signals in someone's body odor that other people would otherwise pick up on.
- **[S13]** Using fragranced cosmetics changes what personality traits raters can accurately infer from body odor: when odor donors used their normal cosmetics/fragrance, the otherwise-reliable link between odor-based ratings of neuroticism and the donor's actual self-reported neuroticism disappeared - while dominance judgments stayed accurate in both conditions. — *peer-reviewed, 2016*, conf high. Quote: "When odor donors could use cosmetics, odor-based perceptions of their neuroticism no longer correlated with self-reports." — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4834355/
  - ELI5: Perfume can hide some honest signals in your natural smell (like how anxious/neurotic you tend to be) while leaving others (like dominance) still readable.
- **[S14]** Skin-resident bacteria (Staphylococcus hominis) actively transport and metabolize odorless sweat-borne precursor molecules, catabolizing them into the volatile, odorous compound 3M3SH - the underlying microbial machinery for individual body odor, and by extension the biochemical backdrop any fragrance lands on. — *reputable-press, 2021*, conf high. Quote: "S. hominis encodes a proton-coupled oligopeptide transporter that imports the thioalcohol-conjugated precursor S-Cys-Gly-3M3SH into the cell, and subsequent catabolism results in the foul-smelling 3M3SH." — https://asm.org/articles/2021/december/microbial-origins-of-body-odor
  - ELI5: Body odor isn't really 'sweat smell' - it's bacteria on your skin eating odorless sweat molecules and turning them into smelly ones, and that same bacterial ecosystem is part of why the same perfume interacts differently with each person.
- **[S15]** The fragrance industry has begun explicitly engineering around skin-microbiome variation - Givaudan's Z-biome technology tests raw perfumery ingredients against common skin bacteria populations, and a competitor (Firmenich/DSM-Firmenich) has launched pro-fragrance molecules that stay dormant until activated by a wearer's own bacterial enzymes (intensifying with warmth/sweat, quieting as skin cools). — *industry, 2021*, conf medium. Quote: "extensive testing of raw perfumery ingredients for their impact on the skin's most common bacteria population." — https://www.givaudan.com/media/trade-media/2021/givaudan-launches-z-biome
  - ELI5: Perfume companies now design fragrances with a person's skin bacteria in mind, and some new fragrance molecules are built to only 'switch on' once your own skin bacteria interact with them.
- **[S16]** Hormonal state measurably shifts a woman's ability to detect and discriminate odors: naturally cycling women in the periovulatory phase (estrogen peak) are more sensitive to social/musk-type odors than women using oral contraceptives; more specifically, naturally cycling women show a 19% decline in body-odor discrimination from follicular to luteal phase, and oral-contraceptive users show a 23% reduction in body-odor discrimination during their withdrawal (placebo) week - in a 36-woman, weekly-tested study. — *peer-reviewed, 2020*, conf high. Quote: "At the transition from follicular to luteal phases, there was a decline of 19% (p = 0.003)... we observed a 23% reduction (p = 0.01) in discrimination accuracy of body odors [withdrawal period]." — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7476921/
  - ELI5: Where you are in your menstrual cycle, or whether you're on the pill, actually changes how well your nose can tell people's natural scents apart - by a fifth to almost a quarter, week to week.
- **[S17]** Wearing personal fragrance measurably degrades the wearer's own subsequent olfactory performance: in a controlled panel study, both a 'just-right' level (3 sprays) and an excessive level (10 sprays) of personal perfume significantly reduced odor detection threshold and discrimination scores relative to no fragrance, though identification ability was unaffected - a real, lab-measured basis for 'nose-blindness' to one's own scent. — *peer-reviewed, 2022*, conf high. Quote: "Even low to moderate levels of personal fragrance can significantly reduce individuals' olfactory capabilities, possibly subsequently altering the perception of test samples during sensory evaluation." — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8834429/
  - ELI5: Wearing your own perfume actually dulls your nose's ability to smell things precisely, even at a normal amount - which is part of why you personally stop noticing your own scent.
