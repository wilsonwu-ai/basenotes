What Olfactif actually is

Olfactif launched around 2013 and bills itself as the original niche fragrance subscription. That claim holds up: it predates most of the current subscription field and built its reputation not on volume or price, but on curation. The company maintains direct relationships with more than 100 independent and niche perfumers — houses like Amouage, D.S. & Durga, État Libre d’Orange, Heretic, Imaginary Authors, Juliette Has a Gun, Memo Paris, Montale, Nasomatto, Orto Parisi, and Roja Dove.

Byrdie named Olfactif “Best subscription box for trendsetters.” Its BBB rating is an A+, which is meaningful in a category where the dominant player (Scentbird) holds an F and thousands of complaints. The brand aesthetic — typography, photography, editorial tone — reads like a boutique beauty brand, not a subscription box. Olfactif’s tagline, “Why smell like everyone else,” is a genuine editorial stance, not hollow positioning copy.

The model is simple, and deliberately so: each month, Olfactif curates a themed box of niche samples. You receive the box they built. You don’t submit a queue, you don’t choose which fragrance you get, and you don’t log into a portal to manage a calendar. You subscribe, and something interesting shows up.

What you actually receive

The standard box is three vials of 2 ml each, for $22 per month. The deluxe box is six vials of 2 ml each, for $40 per month. Commitments run 1, 3, 6, or 12 months, with longer commitments priced lower per month. Shipping is included.

Beyond the samples, Olfactif offers a genuine member perk on full-bottle purchases: 20% off the featured fragrance of the month, 10% off all other fragrances in their store, and 10% off à-la-carte samples. For a subscriber who finds a fragrance they want to own, this discount is real and meaningful. A full bottle of Amouage Interlude Man retails around $450 for 100 ml. A 20% member discount brings that to $360 — a $90 saving on a purchase that the subscription discovery directly made possible.

What Olfactif does genuinely well

We want to be honest about this, because it matters: Olfactif has built something most fragrance subscriptions — including ours — have not.

The niche catalog is real. The 100+ perfumers Olfactif works with are not padded by mass-designer brands. These are genuinely obscure houses that most fragrance subscribers have never sampled, sourced through relationships the company has maintained for over a decade. That is not easy to replicate. Scentbird has scale; Olfactif has depth.

The full-bottle discount changes the math. Most subscription services treat the sample as the product and the full-bottle sale as a nice-to-have. Olfactif structures its model so that the subscription is explicitly designed to lead you toward a bottle purchase at a genuine discount. If discovery-to-ownership is your goal, this is the best-designed funnel in the category for achieving it.

The brand is genuinely premium. This is subjective, but it matters. Fragrance is a luxury category. The packaging, editorial voice, and visual identity should reflect that. Olfactif’s does. Ours is catching up. Scentbird’s and Scentbox’s do not.

The BBB A+ is not nothing. In a category where Scentbird has an F rating and thousands of billing and cancellation complaints, an A+ from the Better Business Bureau signals a company that resolves problems when they arise. The fragrance subscription industry has a trust problem. Olfactif is not part of it.

The curated model: what “you don’t choose” means in practice

This is the point most Olfactif reviews gloss over, and it is the most important thing to understand before subscribing.

Olfactif curates your box. You receive what they decide you receive. In January, they may send three smoky orientals because that fits their winter theme. In March, they may send three green chypres because it aligns with spring. You have no input into either decision.

For a specific type of subscriber — one who genuinely wants to be introduced to houses they have never encountered, who does not have a running list of fragrances to try, and who finds the process of picking from a catalog overwhelming — this is a feature, not a limitation. The curator does the work. You get surprised. That is a real value proposition.

For a different type of subscriber — one who has already spent time on Fragrantica, who has a mental list of Amouage or D.S. & Durga fragrances they want to sample, who wants to try the specific scents they have been reading about — the curated model is a genuine mismatch. You cannot get Amouage Interlude this month if Olfactif decided to curate a floral theme instead. You get what they picked, period.

In our experience, most fragrance subscribers who are reading posts like this one fall into the second category. By the time someone is researching subscription services in any depth, they usually already have opinions and specific scents on their radar. Handing control to a curator is counterintuitive for that buyer, regardless of how good the curator is.

This is where we will admit our own conflict: Base Note is a pick-your-own service. We think most luxury fragrance buyers prefer to choose. But we also think a meaningful segment of the market genuinely prefers curation, and Olfactif serves that segment better than we do. Know which one you are before you subscribe to either.

The 2 ml question: how much fragrance do you actually need?

A 2 ml vial delivers roughly 15 to 20 sprayings. Applied at two sprays per day — a reasonable daily use for most wearers — that is one to two weeks of wear time per vial.

Is that enough? For a niche fragrance that builds complexity over hours and evolves differently across multiple wearings, one to two weeks is a defensible testing window. Fragrance professionals often form opinions on samples smaller than 2 ml. Many experienced buyers would agree that 15 wearings is enough to know whether a fragrance works for them.

The counter-argument is practical: niche fragrances from houses like Amouage or Nasomatto are not the fragrances most people reach for every morning. They are occasion fragrances, statement fragrances, fragrances you wear when it is the right day for that particular mood. At two casual-day wearings per week, 15 sprayings stretches across two months of real-world testing. At that cadence, a 2 ml sample is sufficient.

Where 2 ml becomes an issue is for buyers who want to live with a fragrance daily, the way you would before committing to a full bottle. For that style of testing, the 5 to 8 ml vials offered by Base Note, Scentbird, and Scentbox give you 30 to 100+ sprayings — one to three months of daily wear. If your evaluation style is “I want to wear this every day for a month and see if I still love it,” 2 ml will not answer the question.

We covered the spray-count math in more detail in our post on what Scentbird subscribers switch to, in the context of comparing vial sizes. The short version: more ml means more testing. Whether you need more testing for niche fragrance than for designer fragrance is a real question, and reasonable people disagree on the answer.

Pricing: what $22 buys per ml of fragrance

Fragrance subscriptions are not all priced on the same value metric, which makes direct comparison tricky. Here is the math for Olfactif’s standard box:

  • $22 per month for 3 vials × 2 ml = 6 ml total = $3.67 per ml
  • $40 per month for 6 vials × 2 ml = 12 ml total = $3.33 per ml

For comparison:

  • Base Note: $20 for one 5 ml vial = $4.00 per ml
  • Scentbird (base tier, no surcharge): $16.95 for one 8 ml vial = $2.12 per ml
  • Scentbox: $17.95 for one 8 ml vial = $2.24 per ml

Base Note is slightly more expensive per ml than Olfactif at the standard tier. Scentbird and Scentbox are meaningfully cheaper per ml, but their catalogs do not include the niche houses Olfactif carries, and Scentbird’s luxury-tier surcharges narrow that gap on the specific scents people most want.

The more important pricing consideration for Olfactif subscribers is the full-bottle discount. A subscriber who receives a box, identifies one fragrance they want to own, and uses the 20% member discount to buy it has effectively used the subscription as a try-before-you-buy program at a meaningful long-term saving. At that point, the $22 per month is not just a sample cost — it is an entry fee to a purchasing program that reduces the risk of a $400 bottle decision.

Who should subscribe to Olfactif (and who shouldn’t)

Olfactif is right for you if:

  • You want to discover niche and indie houses you have never encountered, and you are comfortable being surprised rather than choosing
  • You value curation over control — you trust a skilled curator’s taste more than your own ability to navigate a catalog
  • Your end goal is eventually buying full bottles from niche houses, and you want the 20% discount to offset that cost
  • You are a fragrance enthusiast who has already explored designer brands and wants something more obscure and editorial
  • Premium brand experience matters to you at the subscription level (unboxing, editorial, aesthetic)

Olfactif is not right for you if:

  • You have specific fragrances on your radar that you want to try on your timeline
  • You want to rotate through a set of known luxury designer brands (Creed, Tom Ford, Parfums de Marly, Xerjoff) month by month at a predictable price
  • You prefer testing fragrances daily over an extended period rather than wearing niche samples on occasion
  • You want more than 6 ml of fragrance per month without paying $40

FAQ

How does Olfactif’s commitment structure work?

Olfactif offers month-to-month, 3-month, 6-month, and 12-month prepaid commitments. Longer commitments come at a lower effective monthly price. The month-to-month plan gives you the most flexibility but costs the most. The cancellation experience appears to be straightforward and the BBB A+ rating suggests billing problems are not a systemic issue — a meaningful contrast with the cancellation difficulties reported by Scentbird subscribers.

Is 2 ml enough to genuinely evaluate a niche fragrance?

For an experienced fragrance wearer, probably yes — 15 to 20 sprayings is a real testing window for fragrances you plan to wear on occasion rather than daily. For someone who wants to live with a fragrance every day for a month before deciding, 2 ml will run out before you have your answer. The right answer depends on how you actually wear fragrance. If you test niche scents casually across weeks, 2 ml works. If you test by daily wear, it may not.

What kinds of niche houses does Olfactif typically feature?

Olfactif’s catalog skews toward American indie perfumers (D.S. & Durga, Heretic, Imaginary Authors), established European niche houses (Amouage, Memo Paris, Montale, Roja Dove), and provocateur-niche brands (État Libre d’Orange, Nasomatto, Orto Parisi, Juliette Has a Gun). These are not the same houses as the luxury designer brands (Creed, Tom Ford, Parfums de Marly) that Base Note and Scentbird carry. They are generally more experimental, more polarizing, and harder to find in a department store. If you want Creed or Parfums de Marly, Olfactif is the wrong place to look. If you want Orto Parisi or Imaginary Authors, it is the right one.

Is Olfactif worth it as a holiday gift for someone else?

It depends entirely on whether the recipient is a niche fragrance enthusiast who wants to be surprised, or a designer-brand buyer who wants to choose their own scents. If you are buying for someone who already uses words like “chypre” and “fougère” in conversation and who collects bottles from houses most people have never heard of, Olfactif is an excellent gift choice. If you are buying for someone who wears Creed Aventus and wants to explore more of that tier, a subscription that lets them choose — like Base Note — is a better fit. The bottom line

We have tried to write this the same way we would want a competitor to write a review of us: honestly, with the tradeoffs named plainly and the strengths acknowledged without hedging.

Olfactif is a genuinely well-run subscription with a premium brand, real niche perfumer relationships, a clean billing record, and a thoughtful full-bottle discount structure. In the categories it has chosen to compete in — indie and niche discovery, curator-led surprise, brand experience — it is the best-in-class option in the fragrance subscription market.

The limitation is structural, not a flaw in execution: Olfactif is built for a subscriber who trusts a curator more than their own wishlist. That subscriber exists. If that describes you, subscribe.

If what you actually want is to rotate through the luxury designer and niche brands you already know you love — Creed, Tom Ford, Parfums de Marly, Xerjoff, Bond No. 9 — on your own schedule, at a flat price with no surcharges, Base Note is the more direct answer.
