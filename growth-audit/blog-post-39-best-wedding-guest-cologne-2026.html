Thirty-two fragrances are in the rotation right now, and several of the ones named below are among them. So we have an obvious interest in you deciding a wedding is a reason to try something new. If the honest version talks you into two sprays of what you already own, that is a fine outcome.

If you only read one thing: Two sprays. Not four, not “a couple extra because it fades on me.” A wedding is a room full of people standing closer to each other than they normally would, for six hours, often indoors, often warm, with flowers and food already doing scent work in the background. Your job as a guest is to smell good to someone hugging you, and to be undetectable to everyone else. Pick something clean and seasonally appropriate, apply it before you dress, and do not re-apply at the reception.

Why guests over-apply — and why it is not your fault

You stop smelling your own fragrance long before other people do. That is not a figure of speech; it has been measured. A 2022 study in the journal Foods put nineteen participants through the Sniffin’ Sticks olfactory test under three conditions — no fragrance, a “just-about-right” level of perfume, and an excessive one — and concluded that “even low to moderate levels of personal fragrance can significantly reduce individuals’ olfactory capabilities” (Source: Foods, 2022). Odour threshold and odour discrimination both fell significantly in the two perfumed conditions. The detail that matters for a wedding is that the modest dose was enough — you do not have to overdo it to blunt your own nose.

Read that back and the whole over-application problem explains itself. Twenty minutes after you spray, you cannot smell it. You conclude it has worn off. It has not — your nose has adjusted, and everyone who walks up to you gets the full dose you can no longer perceive. The single most useful habit for a wedding is to decide your spray count in advance and refuse to renegotiate it based on what your nose reports later in the day.

The second thing worth knowing before you buy anything: concentration changes the arithmetic. Eau de toilette carries a “fragrance oil concentration ranging between 5-15%,” eau de parfum “around 15-20%,” and extrait de parfum “usually 20-30%” (Source: Ulta Beauty, 2025). Two sprays of an extrait is not the same event as two sprays of an EDT. Every product page on our site lists the concentration — eau de parfum unless marked otherwise — alongside the country of origin, precisely so you can do this maths before the day rather than in the car.

The etiquette, stated plainly

Wedding fragrance etiquette is mostly one idea wearing several hats: you are not the main character. Everything below follows from it.

  • Two sprays, applied at home. One on the chest under the shirt, one on the back of the neck or a wrist. Skip the atomizer in your jacket pocket. The urge to top up at hour four is nose-blindness talking.
  • Do not compete with the flowers. The couple chose those arrangements, paid for them, and will be photographed inches from them. If you are in the wedding party, standing at the altar, or likely to be in close-up photographs, this matters more — a heavy white-floral or a loud gourmand next to a bouquet reads as clashing, not layered. The risk here is volume, not the presence of a floral note: a jasmine sitting in the heart of a scent at two sprays will never fight a bouquet. Six sprays of anything will.
  • Respect a stated request. Some couples ask guests to go fragrance-free, usually because someone close to them has asthma, migraine triggers, or a chemical sensitivity. That is not a preference to negotiate with, and if you are unsure whether it applies to you, ask the couple, not us. Anything beyond “wear none” in that situation is a conversation for a doctor, not a fragrance blog.
  • Ceremony first, reception second. Ceremonies are seated, quiet, often in a small or enclosed space, and frequently the warmest part of the day. Reception rooms are larger, louder, better ventilated and full of competing food smells. Dose for the ceremony, not the dance floor.
  • Test it on a Tuesday, not on the day. Wear the fragrance for a full day at least once before the wedding, in the same weather. This is the specific thing 5 ml is good for.

How many sprays, by venue

Warm air moves scent molecules further and faster — vapour pressure rises steeply with temperature, which is ordinary physics rather than fragrance folklore. An outdoor July ceremony and a February ballroom are not the same problem, and neither is the same as a stone chapel with no airflow.

Season by season

Spring weddings: green, quiet, slightly formal

Spring is garden ceremonies, unpredictable temperature, and a lot of natural floral scent already in the air. You want something that reads as clean and deliberate rather than perfumed.

Creed Green Irish Tweed is the obvious answer and deserves to be: French and lemon verbena over Florentine iris and violet leaves, landing on Mysore sandalwood and ambergris. It is elegant, dated in the good way, and it does not announce itself. Prada Paradigme is the quieter option — Calabrian bergamot and airy musks, green bourbon geranium, then Peru balm, benzoin and guaiac wood. It sits close to the skin, which is exactly the brief. For a brighter, more herbal read, Xerjoff Torino 21 opens on mint, lemon, basil and thyme before settling into lemon verbena and musk.

Summer weddings: the hardest season to get right

Heat is the variable that ruins good intentions. Warm skin pushes a fragrance out faster and further, so the scent you tested in April at two sprays is a different animal in August.

Acqua di Gio Profondo is built for this: sea notes, green mandarin and bergamot up top, rosemary, lavender and cypress in the heart, a mineral-musk-patchouli base. Dolce & Gabbana Light Blue does the citrus version — grapefruit, bergamot, Sicilian mandarin and juniper over pepper and rosemary, finishing on musk, incense and oakmoss. Parfums de Marly Perseus is the dressier of the three, with grapefruit, bergamot and green apple over lavender and geranium. And if the ceremony is outdoors at noon, Rabanne Mesh Metal is almost minimalist — lime and petitgrain bigarade, a single orange blossom heart, ambergris underneath. One spray of it is genuinely enough.

Fall weddings: the season most weddings actually happen in

Autumn gives you the widest range, because the temperature stops working against you and evening receptions start earlier.

Creed Santal is our first recommendation for a daytime fall wedding: juniper, ginger, coriander, rosemary and bergamot over Mysore sandalwood, lavender and orange blossom, into tonka bean, vanilla, white musk and cedarwood. Warm without being loud. For an evening reception, Dolce & Gabbana The One — grapefruit, coriander and basil, then cardamom, ginger and orange blossom, closing on amber, tobacco and cedar — is about as safely handsome as a fall fragrance gets. Givenchy Gentleman takes the more formal route with pepper and lavender over orris and tolu balm, finishing on patchouli and black vanilla.

Parfums de Marly Layton belongs on this list with a caveat attached. Apple, lavender and bergamot into jasmine, violet and geranium, then vanilla, cardamom, sandalwood, pepper and guaiac wood: beautiful, and it carries. One spray, on the chest, under a shirt. Not two.

Winter weddings: the one time richness is correct

Cold air holds scent close, evening ballrooms are formal, and the whole register shifts. This is where the heavier end of the rotation earns its place.

Tom Ford Oud Wood is the safest of the rich options — oud, Brazilian rosewood, sandalwood and cardamom, resolving into amber, tonka bean and vanilla. It is a warm scent that stays unusually close. Parfums de Marly Pegasus is bitter almond, bergamot and heliotrope over lavender and jasmine, then vanilla, sandalwood, amber and guaiac wood: dressed-up without being a dessert. Bvlgari Le Gemme Orom is the smallest note list in the whole rotation — bourbon vanilla, Siam benzoin, agarwood — and the most obviously luxurious. And if you want one fragrance that works at a wedding in any month, Mont Blanc Legend Eau de Parfum does bergamot, grapefruit and lavender over amber, cedarwood and tonka bean, into sandalwood, musk and vetiver. Unexciting is a compliment here.

Six we ship that we would leave at home

We stock all of these and like all of them. None of them is the right call at somebody else’s wedding.

  • Viktor & Rolf Spicebomb Extreme. Cinnamon, cumin and tobacco in the heart. Cumin is a legitimate perfumery material and a bad idea at a seated dinner.
  • Emporio Armani Stronger With You Intensely. Toffee, cinnamon, vanilla, tonka. Excellent on a date; at a reception it competes with the dessert course.
  • Xerjoff Alexandria II. Cumin, civet, oud, wormwood. Genuinely magnificent, and far too much presence for a chapel.
  • Xerjoff Erba Pura. Sweet Sicilian citrus over vanilla and amber. Lovely in spring; in August heat the sweetness escalates.
  • Creed Aventus. Nothing wrong with it, and that is rather the problem. It is worn widely enough that at a 120-person wedding the odds are good someone else has it on, and being recognised as “the Aventus guy” in the receiving line is not the impression you were going for.
  • Versace Eros. Same issue, different room. Mint, green apple and lemon over tonka and vanilla, and one of the most recognisable openings in mainstream fragrance. Save it for a night out where being recognised is the point.

Timing and application

Spray onto clean, moisturised skin about 20 minutes before you get dressed, so the alcohol flashes off and the fabric does not take the hit. Chest and back of the neck beat wrists, because wrists get washed, shaken and gestured with. Do not spray a jacket you will be photographed in, and do not spray your hair.

If it is a destination wedding, or you are travelling at all, a 5 ml atomizer is a much better travel companion than a full bottle — roughly 60 sprays, cabin-bag legal, and nothing to lose at security. That is genuinely how most of our subscribers use theirs.
